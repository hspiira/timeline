1.Coffee exports
Coffee exports in June 2026, totaled to 744,540 60-kilo
bags worth US$ 175.5 million (UGX 651 billion). This
comprised of 668,216 bags of Robusta valued at US$ 147.3
million ( UGX 546 billion) and 76,324 bags of Arabica valued
at US$ 28.2 million (UGX 105 billion) (see Table 1). This was a
decrease of 24.2% and 37.4% in quantity and value respectively
compared to the same month last year (June 2025). Robusta
decreased by 23.9% and 38.7% in quantity and value
respectively, while Arabica exports decreased by 26.8% and
29.6% in quantity and value respectively compared to the same
month last year.
Coffee export volumes during the month were lower than
May 2025. The prices at the global scene also declined with
expectations of improved weather conditions and improved
supply for Brazil and Vietnam.
Coffee exports for twelve months (Finanial Year 2025/26)
totaled 8.3 million 60 kg bags worth US$ 2.18 billion (UGX 7.9
Trillion) compared to 7.7 million 60kg bags worth US$ 2.21
billion (UGX 8.1 trillion) in the previous year (Finanial Year
2024/25). This represents an increase of 7.7% in quantity and
decreaseof 1.2% in value compared to June 2025. The increase in
quantity was due to increased production. The value however
declined as global prices fell due to increased output in Brazil and
Vietnam.

6. Global Situation
World coffee production for 2026/27 is forecast 10.8 million bags
higher than the previous year to a record 189.7 million bags due to
record output in Brazil, Ethiopia, Uganda, and Vietnam. These gains
more than compensate for losses in India and Indonesia. World
coffee bean exports are forecast 11.9 million bags higher to a record
131.4 million on these output gains. Global consumption is expected
to rise to a record 179.7 million bags, with the largest gains occurring
in the European Union and the United States. Ending stocks are
expected to rise for a second consecutive year to 26.3 million bags
((United States Department of Agriculture, Coffee: World
Markets and Trade report-July 2026).

7. Local Situation
During the month of J u n e 2026, farm gate prices ranged from
UGX 5,000-6,000 per kilogram of Kiboko (Robusta dry

Uganda's coffee sub-sector continued to make significant strides
through investments in farmer capacity building, extension services
and regulatory oversight aimed at improving productivity, quality and
sustainability across the coffee value chain.
During the reporting period, 150 farmer training sessions reached
4,741 coffee farmers with practical skills in Good Agronomic Practices
(GAPs), agrochemical safety, traceability and coffee marketing. In
addition, 271 farm visits provided tailored technical support to more
than 907 farmers, helping improve coffee establishment,
rehabilitation of ageing plantations, post-harvest handling and
climate-resilient farming practices.
Efforts to strengthen governance and compliance also intensified. A
total of 252 Value Chain Actors including 125 buying stores, 108
hullers, 2 roasters, 4 wash stations, 3 grading facilities and 10 new
factories were inspected to enforce quality standards, strengthen
traceability and enhance compliance with sector regulations.
To improve market readiness, 10 training and sensitization sessions
equipped 474 coffee traders and processors with knowledge on the
European Union Deforestation Regulation (EUDR), traceability
systems and coffee quality control, positioning Uganda's coffee
industry to meet evolving international market requirements.
Regulatory oversight remained strong, with 304 Value Chain Actors
(155 Coffee hullers, 148 traders/Coffee buying stores and 1 wash
station) licensed during the period, reinforcing compliance,
accountability and the sustainable growth of Uganda's coffee sector.

held in Brussels, Belgium from 25th to 27th June 2026 (Organized
by MAAIF and Uganda Mission in Brussels. Uganda was this year's
event portrait country and launched the Uganda coffee Brand;
"UGANDA COFFEE, IT'S IN OUR NATURE” which was presided over
by the State Minister for Agriculture together with Permanent
Secretaries of Ministry of Agriculture Animal Industry and
Fisheries, Ministry Of Foreign Affairs and Ministry of Finance,
Planning and Economic Development, Head of Mission, BENELUX,
and other dignitaries. Over 20 coffee exporters participated and
showcased their coffee products and brands. Uganda held cupping
sessions to showcase its coffee's unique attributes and cup profiles
according to the 11 Agro-Ecological Zones. The Expo goers were
given a chance to taste Uganda Coffee where over 5,000 cups
were served.
